Each recipient signs from their own private link. They need no account.

## Open the invitation

The invitation email holds a link to the signing page. When mail is not set up, the sender
shares the private link and verification code directly. The page shows the document title
and who the request is for. If an earlier recipient has not signed yet, the page says **Waiting for
your turn.** and **Check status** refreshes it.

## Enter the access code

If the sender set an access code for you, the page first asks for it under **Enter your access
code**. The sender gives you the code separately, never in the invitation email. Enter it and
choose **Continue**; the page then moves on to the email or verification code. Each wrong code
shows how many attempts remain. After five wrong codes the page reads **This invitation is
locked.** and only the sender can start again with a new envelope.

## Confirm the email address

With mail set up, recipients verify access to the invited mailbox before viewing or signing:

1. Choose **Email me a code**.
2. Enter the six-digit code from the separate verification email and choose
   **Verify code**.

When mail is not set up, enter the six-digit verification code the sender shared with your
link and choose **Verify code**. The code lasts 10 minutes. If it expires, ask the sender
for a new link and code; the old link will stop working.

## Agree to the disclosure

Before the document opens, the page shows the **Electronic record and signature disclosure**:
what signing electronically means, how to get a paper copy from the sender, how to withdraw
consent, how to keep your contact details current, and the browser and software you need.
Tick *I have read the electronic record and signature disclosure and agree to receive
documents and sign electronically.* and choose **Agree and continue**. Inkwell records the
disclosure's version and the time you agreed, and the certificate quotes that version. You
agree once per envelope; if the disclosure changes, the page shows the new version.

## Review and sign

1. Read every page of the document. Each field for this recipient is outlined on its page.
2. Under **Your fields**, type a full name into each signature field and fill any text or
   date fields. The typed name is what Inkwell places into the PDF.
3. Tick the consent box: *I have reviewed this document and consent to electronic
   signatures. I intend my entries to be my legally binding signature.*
4. Choose **Sign document**.

The page then shows **Your signature has been recorded.** When everyone has signed, the
same link offers **Download executed PDF** and **Download certificate**.

## Download or print the document

Once you have agreed to the disclosure, **Download PDF** saves the exact document you are
asked to sign and **Print** opens your browser's print dialog for it. Both stay available
after you sign, while the others finish; once everyone has signed, download the executed
PDF and certificate instead.

## Sign on paper

When the sender allows it, the page offers **Sign on paper instead…**. Download or print the
document, sign it by hand, then upload a scan (PDF) or a phone photo (JPEG or PNG) of the
signed pages, up to 2 MiB, with **Upload paper signature**. The page then reads **Your paper
signature is with the sender.** If the sender accepts it, it counts as your wet-ink signature
and the next recipient is invited; if not, you get an email with the reason and can upload
a new scan or sign electronically. When the sender has not allowed paper signing, the option
does not appear.

## Decline to sign

A recipient whose turn it is can refuse instead: choose **Decline to sign…**, give a reason
and choose **Decline**. The envelope stops at once. Nobody later in the order is invited,
the sender and everyone who already signed get an email with the reason, and the page then
reads **This envelope was declined.** The sender's copy of the envelope keeps the reason and
a decline certificate recording who declined, when and why.

## Withdraw consent

After agreeing to the disclosure and before signing, a recipient can choose **Withdraw
consent…** and then **Withdraw consent**. Like a decline, this stops the envelope at once:
nobody later in the order is invited, the sender and everyone who already signed get an
email, and the page reads **Consent to sign electronically was withdrawn.** The certificate
records the withdrawal and the disclosure version the recipient had agreed to. Withdrawing
does not undo a signature already given.

## Keep the link private

The invitation link grants access to that signing request. Recipients should not forward
it. If an invitation expires or the envelope is voided, the page says so and the
recipient should ask the sender for a new one.
