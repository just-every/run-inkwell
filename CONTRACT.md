# Inkwell contract

Target edition: 17 October 2026. This contract describes the product Inkwell is being built towards and marks how close the current code is (see [Status](#status)). It is a target, not a release verdict: `ACCEPTANCE.md` lists every clause's acceptance evidence and decides acceptance. Clause IDs never change. This documentation round changes no homepage, interface or runtime behavior.

## Product target

Inkwell is the owned electronic signature platform a US business buys instead of Docusign eSignature, Adobe Acrobat Sign or Dropbox Sign: reusable templates, any number of recipients with flexible routing, automatic reminders and real delivery operations, bulk send and public signing forms, signer identity options, an API with embedded signing and completion webhooks, and completed documents pushed back to the CRM, file store and contract repository the business already runs on. It is installed on the buyer's own Cloudflare account, with no per-envelope or per-seat fees.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Category | Electronic signature software |
| Incumbents a buyer switches from | Docusign eSignature, Adobe Acrobat Sign, Dropbox Sign |
| Up-and-comers it must also beat | Documenso, DocuSeal |
| First buyer | An operations manager at a US service business of 10 to 100 people who sends recurring customer, supplier and employee agreements (service agreements, NDAs, W-9 and onboarding packets, offer letters); costs in USD, no per-envelope pricing, the buyer's own Cloudflare account |
| What switching means | Their templates, completed envelopes and certificates arrive from the incumbent with an explicit list of what did not map; every daily job (prepare, route, chase, sign, file, report) has an Inkwell equivalent; their CRM, file store, mailbox, automation and payment accounts plug in; their admins keep roles, SSO, branding, audit and API access |

A buyer replacing an incumbent needs the whole job, so the target is the whole job:

- **Prepare.** PDF and Word uploads, multi-document envelopes, the complete field set with validation, conditional and calculated fields, AI field and recipient detection, text tags and prefill from business data, document generation from Word templates, free-form signing (`PREP-*`), copying an earlier envelope, and reusable shared templates with saved roles, combined several to an envelope and matched on upload (`TMPL-*`).
- **Route.** Signing your own document with nobody else involved; any number of signers, approvers, form fillers, witnesses, editors, specify-recipients agents, viewers and copy recipients, each seeing only their own documents; sequential, parallel, mixed and conditional order; signing groups, delegation, in-person signing and a contact book (`ROUTE-*`).
- **Sign.** Guided, accessible, mobile-first signing with drawn, typed or uploaded signatures and initials, ESIGN/UETA consumer disclosure, decline, download, print and paper signing, comments and supplemental documents, branding and Spanish (`SIGNX-*`), with email code, access code, SMS code, knowledge-based and phone authentication and provider identity verification (`IDV-*`), and payment at signing through Stripe (`INTEG-010`).
- **Follow through.** Delivery status, bounce handling, automatic reminders, configurable expiry, resend by email or SMS, envelope correction, completed copies to every party and sender notifications (`DELIV-*`).
- **Scale.** Bulk send from a spreadsheet and public signing web forms (`BULK-001`, `FORM-001`).
- **Keep the record.** An envelope dashboard with search, folders, custom fields, reports and a per-recipient timeline (`MANAGE-*`); a certificate of completion with per-recipient evidence, a sealed PDF, public verification and retention rules (`RECORD-*`).
- **Administer.** Roles, custom permission profiles, envelope sharing, SSO and SCIM, two-factor sign-in, account defaults, an account audit log, transparent usage and a first-day readiness check (`ADMIN-*`), with optional compliance profiles (`COMPLY-*`).
- **Connect.** A REST API, webhooks, embedded signing and sending, SDKs and a test mode (`API-*`); HubSpot, Salesforce, Orbit, Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, Box, Dropbox, Chest, Locket, Zapier, Make, Power Automate, n8n and Crank (`INTEG-*`).
- **Anywhere.** The responsive web app and native iOS and Android apps (`MOBILE-*`).
- **Move in and out.** Docusign, Adobe Acrobat Sign, Dropbox Sign, Documenso and DocuSeal imports with ownership carried over, throttled and resumable at real account size, cutover of in-flight envelopes, API compatibility for common calls, and complete exports (`MOVE-*`, `PORT-*`).
- **Push further.** Agents that assemble the envelope from CRM and email context, fill what is missing, chase stalled signers under the sender's rules, verify that every party signed the intended revision and then update the CRM, contract repository and next task (`AGENT-*`).

### How to read this contract

- **Published clauses** are the clauses under [Shipped guarantees](#shipped-guarantees). The seed's previous contract and ACCEPTANCE.md first-release jobs already promised them; they are restated here with IDs as one part of the full job. Each states the guarantee that holds for every envelope and names today's build as the narrowed or default case of the target clauses it references.
- **Precedence.** Where a published clause names a narrowed case (first-release roles, ad hoc approval, five ordered signers, email codes, one signer per order) and a target clause generalizes it, the target clause governs, and no acceptance journey asserts that the narrowed case is the only allowed behavior. The guarantees that never narrow are: authorization on every request, a named authority for every send, fingerprinted bytes that never change after sending, changes only through audited corrections that never touch a signed field, consent before signing, and routing order.
- **Target clauses** are under [Target clauses](#target-clauses) and **push-further clauses** under [Push further](#push-further). They are binding product requirements that a build round works towards.
- **Status** is `done`, `partial` or `missing`, judged from the code and tests at the commit named in [Status](#status). `done` means the behavior ships and a cited test exercises it; `partial` means some of it ships, or there is code without a requirement-level test; `missing` means there is no implementation. Status is a build-planning judgment, not acceptance.
- Every acceptance journey runs through a public door: the browser at 1440 and 390 pixels wide, the HTTP API, a signing link, or a provider fake at the paid or network edge (mail, SMS, identity, payment, CRM, storage). Live provider receipt is recorded separately and never inferred from a fake.

## Shipped guarantees

### Routing and access

- **HTTP-001 — Health, API and document routing.** `/health` includes deployment identity; Hono owns `/api/*`, and Start serves application documents. Unknown routes return 404. **Acceptance evidence:** the built Worker answers `/health` with its deployment fingerprint, source and version, serves the application document, and returns 404 for unknown routes.

- **ACCESS-001 — Server-side roles and scoped authority.** Private API handlers authorize the session, API key or agent token and its role, permission profile or scopes on every request, against the role matrix of `ADMIN-001` and any custom permission profile (`ADMIN-008`): senders prepare, send, correct and void their own envelopes and those shared with them for editing; admins act on every envelope; viewers read; only the owner manages agent tokens, archives and erasure. A preparation token may only prepare (`DELEGATE-001`). Changes require the application's own origin. Every write rechecks the actor's current role, session and the record's version in the same atomic D1 batch. A Base member is Inkwell's sender or viewer, a viewer until an owner or admin chooses sender, so members kept their read-only authority when these roles shipped; a sender sees and acts on the envelopes they created and those shared with them to edit, reads those shared with them to view, and every other envelope is refused with 403 and absent from their list and search (`ADMIN-001`); every role may sign their own turn (`MANAGE-003`). Member roles are installation state and do not travel in an archive, so a restored member is a viewer. Every write by a sender rechecks, in the same atomic batch, that they created the envelope or still hold an edit share of it. Folder and group shares (`ADMIN-001`), permission profiles (`ADMIN-008`), API keys (`API-001`) apply this matrix before they ship, and correction (`DELIV-006`) applies it: whoever may change the envelope corrects it, and a viewer is refused. **Acceptance evidence:** signed-out, viewer, sender, admin, owner and agent-token requests to every document, import, agent and archive door return the allow or refuse that `ADMIN-001`'s matrix documents; a sender-role member prepares, sends and voids their own envelope and is refused on another sender's unshared envelope; a cross-origin change is refused.

### Sending and signing

- **SEND-001 — Prepare a fixed envelope and authorize sending.** A sender prepares an envelope whose documents are bound by their SHA-256 fingerprints, with recipients, routing order and fields (`PREP-*`, `ROUTE-001`). Every send has a named authority, recorded on the envelope. An ad hoc send by a person requires that person's explicit confirmation that the document is eligible for electronic signature. A send from a template, a bulk batch (`BULK-001`), a web form (`FORM-001`), an API key or OAuth client with the send scope (`API-001`, `MOVE-007`), an integration (`INTEG-001`, `INTEG-002`, `INTEG-011`) or a schedule (`PREP-009`) goes out under the account's sending policy (`ADMIN-004`), which the owner or an admin set once: there is no per-envelope human approval, and the record names the policy, its version and the credential that used it. An internal approval rule (`ROUTE-004`) adds a person's approval wherever the policy demands one. After sending, the documents' bytes never change, and recipients, fields and settings change only through audited corrections (`DELIV-006`, `ROUTE-006`, `ROUTE-011`), which never alter a field already signed or a signature already given. Today's build is the narrow first-release case: one PDF, one to five ordered signers, ad hoc sends by a person only, and audited corrections by `DELIV-006`; that case is a subset of `ROUTE-001` (up to 100 recipients) and the policies above, never a limit on them. **Acceptance evidence:** prepare an envelope through the API; an ad hoc approval without the eligibility confirmation is refused and one with it sends; a `FORM-001` submission and an `API-001` send-scope call each produce a sent envelope with no human approval step, and each record names the sending policy and credential that allowed it; with the policy for API sends turned off, the same call is refused; after signer 1 signs, a `DELIV-006` correction of signer 2's email succeeds and is audited, while any change to signer 1's signed field, direct or through a correction, is refused.

- **SIGN-001 — Authenticated, consented signing in routing order.** Each recipient opens their own single-use link and passes the authentication method the sender chose for them (`IDV-001`): none (the link alone), email code, access code, SMS code, knowledge-based or phone authentication, or identity verification. A signed-in member signing in the app is authenticated by their session (`ROUTE-009`, `MANAGE-003`). The email-code method sends a six-digit code with one-minute resend spacing, ten-minute expiry and five attempts. The recipient reviews the exact approved bytes of the documents visible to them (`ROUTE-012`), completes exactly their fields and consents explicitly (`SIGNX-005`) before signing. Only recipients whose routing order is current can sign: recipients sharing an order act in parallel, and the next order is invited when everyone at the current order has finished (`ROUTE-002`). Today's build offers link only and email code per recipient, each with or without an access code (`IDV-001`); when mail is suppressed every remote recipient verifies a sender-provided verification code alongside the owner's once-visible signing link, and link only is refused. **Acceptance evidence:** two email-code signers verify codes, reject a wrong code, cannot sign out of turn, consent and sign in order, and the second invitation arrives only after the first signature; a link-only recipient signs with no code; two order-1 signers are invited together and sign in parallel, and order 2 is invited only after both have signed.

- **SEND-002 — Void an unfinished envelope.** The envelope's sender, anyone it is shared with for editing, or an admin (`ADMIN-001`) voids a sent or partially signed envelope with a reason; outstanding links, codes and signer sessions stop working immediately and pending mail is canceled. Owners, admins, the sender who created the envelope and anyone it is shared with to edit (`ADMIN-001`) void it. **Acceptance evidence:** a sender-role member voids their own envelope after sending and is refused on another sender's unshared envelope; the voided envelope's invitation link refuses and the pending message is canceled.

- **DELEGATE-001 — Preparation agents never send.** The owner issues and revokes scoped agent tokens. An agent prepares an envelope through the API; an envelope a preparation token prepares is sent only by a signed-in person's approval, and both preparation and approval are in the audit trail with their actors. A revoked token loses all authority. Credentials that send without a person are a separate grant, never a preparation token: API keys and OAuth clients with the send scope, used under the account's sending policy (`API-001`, `ADMIN-004`, `SEND-001`). **Acceptance evidence:** an agent token prepares an envelope but cannot approve, sign or read archives, and fails after revocation; the audit trail names the agent and the approving person.

### Execution records

- **RECORD-001 — Executed PDF, certificate and verifiable audit trail.** On the last signature Inkwell produces the executed PDF with the signed values, a completion certificate and a hash-chained audit trail; the sender downloads the original, executed PDF, certificate, audit JSON and a handoff manifest, and verification recomputes every fingerprint and the event chain. Signers download the executed PDF and certificate after completion. **Acceptance evidence:** complete an envelope, download every artifact, and show recomputed document and audit fingerprints agree with the stored values and the handoff manifest.

### Moving in, backup and erasure

- **MOVE-001 — DocuSeal archive move-in.** The owner previews a DocuSeal completed-submission archive without writes, approves its fingerprint, imports signed PDFs and provider evidence with their provider provenance, reads the durable import journal, downloads imported artifacts and undoes the import. **Acceptance evidence:** dry run, fingerprint approval, import, journal, downloads and undo of the DocuSeal fixture, with custody preserved at every step.

- **PORT-001 — Whole-installation export and restore.** The owner exports a checksum-bound archive of all envelopes, artifacts and import journals and restores it only into an empty installation. **Acceptance evidence:** export validates its contents, a populated installation refuses restore, and a fresh installation restores the executed file, certificate and full import journal.

- **ERASE-001 — Erasure with a restore fence.** The owner erases a voided, expired or completed native envelope after typing ERASE; its files, recipients, mail and command results are removed, and the later erasure journal stops an older backup from restoring it. **Acceptance evidence:** erase an envelope, show its files and identity custody are gone, and show a fresh-store restore with the later journal refuses resurrection while restoring everything else.

## Target clauses

### Documents and fields

- **PREP-001 — PDF and Word uploads at business size.** A sender uploads PDF, DOCX, DOC, RTF, TXT, PNG and JPEG sources up to 25 MB each; non-PDF files are converted to PDF by an isolated conversion service before preparation, and the converted PDF is what every signer sees and what the envelope's fingerprint binds. Encrypted, corrupt or unsupported files are refused with a reason. Files are stored in object storage, not in database rows. Each converted upload is matched against templates (`TMPL-005`) before this ships. **Acceptance evidence:** upload a 20 MB PDF, a DOCX with tables and images, and a phone photo of a page; each converts, previews and signs with a fingerprint over the converted PDF; an encrypted PDF and a 30 MB file are refused with their reasons.

- **PREP-002 — Multi-document envelopes.** One envelope holds up to 20 documents and 100 MB in total, in an order the sender sets; fields reference a document and page, signers sign all of them in one session, and the executed record keeps each document separately and as one combined PDF. A document can be marked optional to sign or view-only. The envelope detail page (`MANAGE-005`) lists every document in order. Each added document is matched against templates (`TMPL-005`), and an applied match places its fields on that document, before this ships. **Acceptance evidence:** send an envelope with a service agreement, a W-9 and a view-only policy; two signers complete it in one session each; the record contains three executed documents, a combined PDF and one certificate covering all three fingerprints.

- **PREP-003 — The complete field set with validation.** Fields include signature, initials, date signed (filled automatically in the signer's US time zone), full name, email, company, title, text, number, checkbox, checkbox group with minimum and maximum, radio group, dropdown, attachment upload, stamp, and a note; each can be required or optional, read-only or editable, and validated by pattern (US phone, ZIP, SSN or EIN masked on display, email, currency in USD, date format). Each business-data field type added here inherits PREP-008’s prefill, reported source and read-only obligations before it ships. **Acceptance evidence:** a template with every field type is signed on desktop and phone; invalid ZIP, phone and EIN values are refused with field-level messages; attachments land in the record; masked values are hidden in the dashboard but present in the executed PDF; a signer adopts drawn initials and they appear in every initials field of the executed PDF, with the method on the certificate. Initials fields carry `SIGNX-002`'s obligation: before they ship, the signer adopts initials by typing, drawing or uploading them, the adopted initials go on every initials field, and the record and certificate keep the method. Every new field type joins `SIGNX-003`'s accessibility scan and its keyboard-only and screen-reader journeys before it ships. Round A adds initials, date signed, full name, email, company and title. Date signed is generated from the server signing instant in the signer-selected US zone (Eastern, Central, Mountain, Arizona, Pacific, Alaska or Hawaii); sender prefill and caller-supplied dates cannot replace it. Business-data fields accept PREP-008 prefill and reported sources with server-enforced read-only values; signatures, initials and generated dates are not business-data prefills. Validation checks syntax, not identity or tax registration: ZIP is five digits with an optional four-digit suffix, US phone is ten digits with an optional country code 1 and conventional separators, SSN is nine digits or XXX-XX-XXXX, EIN is nine digits or XX-XXXXXXX, email needs a local part and dotted domain, and dates are real YYYY-MM-DD calendar dates. SSN and EIN displays are always masked; other business fields may be masked by the sender. Masking changes field display on preparation/signing overlays and dashboard values, not the executed PDF or the existing authorized audit downloads. Initials are adopted separately from a signature and the same initials and mark apply to every filled initials field; a signed-in member's saved initials are offered on subsequent in-app signing, outside archives like their saved signature. Round B's choice/note slice adds radio groups, dropdowns, checkbox groups and single-line notes. Radio and dropdown values are one of the sender's exact option labels. A checkbox group's value is a JSON array of distinct declared labels, stored in declaration order and printed as a comma-separated list; minimum and maximum selection counts are inclusive whole numbers from zero to the option count. An answered group meets both bounds; a required group also needs at least one selection, while an optional group may be left blank. Choices declare 2–20 distinct single-line labels, each at most 100 characters and together at most 500 characters as JSON. Notes use the existing single-line text value and optional validation/masking. Each of these four types accepts sender/API prefill with a reported source and a server-enforced read-only setting, survives templates, copies and archive restore, and joins PREP-003's desktop/phone keyboard, accessibility-tree and light/dark scan journey. Image stamps also accept PNG or JPEG uploads of at most 512 KiB, using SIGNX-002’s actual image parser and size bound. Stamps accept sender/API prefill with a reported source and a read-only flag; the API value is a data URL containing the image bytes, kept with the field values through copies, templates and archives, protected and erased with the envelope. The executed PDF scales the image to fit its field without changing its proportions. Signing and preparation show an image preview unless masked; the dashboard shows an uploaded-image label, never its encoded bytes. A stamp is a supplied image, not a verified seal. This is an implementation slice; attachment fields remain required by the full clause.

- **PREP-004 — Conditional and calculated fields.** A field can be shown, required or hidden based on another field's value (for example, "Other" reveals a text field), and a number field can be calculated from others (totals in USD). Hidden fields are never required and never printed. The inspector sets a field's visibility condition, independent required setting or required-only-when condition, and a number field's sum, first-minus-the-rest or product over named number fields. Conditions compare trimmed text, a checkbox's true/false or an exact USD number; references stay within one recipient, cannot refer to signatures and cannot form cycles. Fields are required when shown unless marked optional (checkboxes default optional); a required checkbox must be checked. A required-only-when rule overrides that setting while the field stays visible. At least one visible signature must be completed. USD inputs allow up to two decimal places and safe integer cents; outputs keep two decimals, print with a dollar sign, and products round once to the nearest cent, halfway away from zero. Hidden numeric inputs contribute zero to formulas; a missing shown input leaves the result incomplete. Calculated fields are read-only and recomputed by the server; hidden submitted values are discarded. The same rules survive draft editing, templates and copying. **Acceptance evidence:** a signer checks a box that reveals two required fields, then unchecks it and finishes without them; a calculated total updates live and prints in the executed PDF. Test `PREP-004` in `tests/api.test.mjs` asserts visible required values and required-only-when values, optional omission, invalid references/cycles/formulas and USD inputs, recomputed sum/difference/product and hidden data absent from the record and executed PDF. `tests/journeys/conditional-fields.mjs` configures conditions and a formula in the inspector, checks and unchecks the box, changes a live USD total and completes signing with the keyboard at 1440 and 390 pixels; the inspector and signing page join `SIGNX-003`'s accessibility scan, and the accessibility tree exposes the conditional fields.

- **PREP-005 — Direct field placement and editing.** The sender places fields by click or drag on page thumbnails and the full page, resizes, duplicates, aligns and moves them between signers, assigns them by color, and sets each field's properties; keyboard users place and move fields with arrow keys. The exact semantics: positions and sizes are fractions of the page from its top left corner, kept to 0.0001, and every edit keeps the field whole on its page; a click on a page or its thumbnail places the field there, Enter on a focused page or thumbnail places it at the page's center, and the field chip can be dragged onto either; on a field’s focused selection checkbox the arrow keys move it by 1% of the page, Shift with the arrow keys resizes it by 1% (Right and Down grow, Left and Up shrink; never under 3% wide or 2% high), Enter or Space toggles it in the selection and Delete removes it; a field dragged onto another page or that page's thumbnail moves there; Duplicate copies the selection just below itself and Duplicate to every page copies it to the same place on every other page; align lines two or more selected fields up on the left, center, right, top, middle or bottom of the box around them all; each recipient's fields take that recipient's color (six colors, repeating from the seventh recipient); the inspector sets a selected field's recipient, label, type, page, place and size, and on a phone a field is drawn at least 24 pixels square while its saved box stays exact. The PDF overlays are pointer editing targets; the equivalent non-overlapping field list is the keyboard and screen-reader editing surface, including arrow-key movement and Shift+arrow resizing. Each property a later clause adds to a field (`PREP-003`'s initials, checkbox, radio, dropdown, attachment, number and pattern types, required and read-only flags, `PREP-004`'s conditions and formulas, `PREP-011`'s signer-placed fields) is set in this inspector, with a keyboard way, before it ships. **Acceptance evidence:** at 1440 and 390 pixels, place twelve fields across three pages by pointer and by keyboard, duplicate one to every page, reassign two to another signer, and save without losing a field. Drag, resize, duplicate, align and reassign join `SIGNX-003`'s accessibility scan and keyboard-only journey (with a keyboard way to do each) before they ship.

- **PREP-006 — AI field and recipient detection.** On upload, Inkwell proposes fields (signature lines, dates, name and title blocks, checkboxes, initials on every page) and recipients (parties named in the document, matched to contacts and CRM records), each with its location and confidence, using the buyer's configured AI provider. The sender accepts, edits or rejects each proposal; nothing is sent from a proposal alone. **Acceptance evidence:** upload the unmarked two-party service agreement fixture, whose two signature lines, two date lines and two printed-name lines sit at recorded page coordinates; all six are proposed, each on the right page and party, with its center within 12 points (one sixth of an inch) of the recorded position and its width and height within 25% of the recorded box; no signature field is proposed away from a recorded position; the sender rejects one, accepts the rest and the envelope records which fields came from detection.

- **PREP-007 — Text tags and existing PDF form fields.** Text tags in the document (for example `{{signature:1}}` or Docusign-style anchor strings) and existing AcroForm fields become Inkwell fields automatically, with their names kept for API prefill. **Acceptance evidence:** a DOCX with text tags and a fillable IRS W-9 PDF both prepare with no manual placement, and the W-9's named fields prefill through the API.

- **PREP-008 — Prefill from business data.** Fields can be filled by the sender before sending or from an API payload, editable by the signer or locked as read-only; each prefilled value records its reported source. Today this covers text, date, checkbox and USD number fields. Signatures and calculated fields cannot be prefilled. The inspector sets the value, source and lock in drafts and templates; template.use accepts a prefill map keyed by field ID, with value, source and optional readOnly. Values are one trimmed line of at most 500 characters, source labels one line of at most 200; dates use valid YYYY-MM-DD, checkboxes true/false, and USD values follow PREP-004’s exact safe-cent semantics. Missing signer inputs use the prepared defaults; an editable value can be replaced or cleared, while an attempt to change a visible locked value is refused by signing and save-progress, even through direct transport. Hidden fields stay discarded under PREP-004. The prepared field definition and audit trail bind the supplied value and source; the signed record separately records the signer’s final values. Provenance is labeled “supplied by the caller, not verified with the CRM”, never as provider verification. Prefill and sources follow the existing verified-document and disclosure gates and are absent from an unverified recipient view. Defaults, sources and locks survive draft edits, templates, copies and whole-installation restore, without copying signed entries. Native CRM record merging belongs to INTEG-001, INTEG-002 and INTEG-003: each merges the selected record into these fields and records its source before it ships. **Acceptance evidence:** send from a template with a HubSpot-fake deal supplied as the API payload; company, address and amount prefill with their reported source in the record, and the signer cannot change the locked amount. Test PREP-008 in tests/prefill.test.mjs asserts defaults and editable overrides, direct lock refusal, provenance, gated reads, invalid input refusal, executed PDF values, draft/template/copy custody and fresh archive restore. tests/journeys/prefill.mjs configures template and draft prefill in the inspector and completes accessible keyboard signing at 1440 and 390 pixels, with sources in the accessibility tree and a read-only USD amount.

- **PREP-009 — Messages, drafts and scheduled sending.** The sender sets the email subject and message for the envelope and a private message per recipient, saves a draft and returns to it, and schedules sending for a future date and time in a US time zone. A draft is a prepared envelope before approval; reopened, everything in it can be changed and saved again (its PDF, title, recipients and routing order, access codes, fields, expiry, reminders, copy recipients, the paper option, the subject, the message and each private message), keeping its ID, sender and audit trail, and the record names every saved version. An access code is kept only for the same recipient in the same place in the list; elsewhere it is entered again. The subject (one line, up to 200 characters) replaces "Please sign: <title>"; the message (up to 2,000 characters) goes to every recipient, and a private message (up to 1,000) only into that recipient's invitation and their verified signing page. A copy (`PREP-010`) keeps the messages. A schedule is set at approval as a wall-clock time in one of the US zones America/New_York, America/Chicago, America/Denver, America/Phoenix, America/Los_Angeles, America/Anchorage or Pacific/Honolulu, in the future and before expiry; a time that does not exist in that zone is refused, and scheduling needs mail set up. The person's eligibility approval with that time is the send's authority, recorded as approved for later; the first invitations go on the first hourly pass at or after it, nobody can sign before then, and reminders and expiry warnings count from the send. Scheduled sends under the account's sending policy join with `ADMIN-004` (`SEND-001`). **Acceptance evidence:** save a draft, reopen it, add private messages, schedule it for the next business day at 9:00 America/Chicago and show it sends then with each recipient seeing only their own private message.

- **PREP-010 — Copy an envelope.** From any sent, completed, declined, voided or expired envelope the sender can see, Copy creates a new draft with the same documents (the original, unsigned bytes), recipients, fields, messages and settings, without any entered values or signatures; the new envelope records the envelope it was copied from. **Acceptance evidence:** copy a completed envelope; the draft has the unsigned documents, the same recipients and fields and no signed values; send it, and its record names the source envelope.

- **PREP-011 — Free-form signing.** When the account's policy allows it (`ADMIN-004`), a sender sends documents with no placed fields; each signer then places their own signature, initials, date, name and text fields wherever they choose, must place at least one signature before finishing, and the record and certificate mark the envelope as free-form and list the fields each signer placed. **Acceptance evidence:** send a one-page letter with no fields to one signer; the signer places a signature and a date on page 1 and finishes, and the executed PDF shows both at the places chosen; finishing with no signature placed is refused; with the policy off, sending without fields is refused in the UI and API.

- **PREP-012 — Document generation from Word templates.** A template's DOCX can carry merge fields, repeating table rows (for example line items with quantities and USD amounts) and conditional sections. At send time Inkwell fills them from sender input, a CRM record (`PREP-008`) or the API payload, converts the result to PDF (`PREP-001`) and fingerprints the generated document; the sender previews it before sending, and a missing required merge value stops the send with its name. Clause libraries and negotiated drafting stay in Locket. **Acceptance evidence:** through the API, generate from a DOCX template with a client-name merge field, a line-item table fed three rows with a calculated USD total, and a section included only when a payload flag is true; the preview and the executed PDF show the name, three rows, the total and the section, and a second send with the flag false omits the section; a payload missing the client name is refused before sending, naming the field.

### Templates

- **TMPL-001 — Reusable templates with saved roles.** A template holds documents, fields, roles (for example Client and Company Signer) with routing order, messages, reminders, expiry and identity settings; sending from it asks only for each role's name and email (or a signing group, `ROUTE-005`) and any sender fields. Several templates can be combined in one envelope (`TMPL-004`). Today a template holds one PDF (`PREP-002` adds documents to templates before it ships) and one to five roles, each with a name unique in the template, a routing order (roles that share one sign at the same time, `ROUTE-002`), an optional private message and whether it needs an access code (`IDV-003`); the fields placed for each role, with at least one signature field per role; the invitation subject and message; reminders (`DELIV-003`); expiry as whole days from preparation, 1 to 365 and 120 unless set (`DELIV-004`); and the paper (`SIGNX-008`) and reassignment (`ROUTE-006`) options. Its identity settings are the email code every recipient verifies today and the access-code requirement: an access code is a secret the sender gives that person, so it is never stored in the template and the send form asks for it beside that role's name and email; Each role keeps `IDV-001`'s method (email code or link only). There are no sender fields today; `PREP-008`'s sender-filled fields join the send form before they ship. Sending prepares a draft from the template's stored bytes, validated exactly as a hand-prepared envelope, and the envelope and its prepared event record the template's ID and name; a person then approves it with the eligibility confirmation, as for an ad hoc send, until `ADMIN-004`'s template sending policy ships (`SEND-001`). Owners, admins and senders make templates from the prepare screen; a sender sees, uses and deletes the templates they made, owners and admins all of them, and viewers none (`ACCESS-001`'s envelope rule); `TMPL-002`'s folders and shares let a sender use or edit others' templates. Editing a template makes its next version (`TMPL-003`); deleting a template leaves envelopes sent from it unchanged. Templates are business records and travel in the archive. **Acceptance evidence:** create a template with three roles, send it twice to different people, and show each envelope inherited fields, routing, reminders and expiry with no placement.

- **TMPL-002 — Shared team templates.** Templates live in personal or shared folders with an owner, can be shared with people or groups to use or to edit, and are searchable by name and tag. Exact semantics: every template is in exactly one folder: its maker's personal folder ("My templates", only theirs) or a shared folder, which owners, admins and senders create and own. A template's owner is its maker and a folder's is its maker. Its owner, owners and admins share a template or folder with an active sender, found by email, to use (see and send from it) or to edit (also save its next version, `TMPL-003`); sharing again changes the access and removing a share takes it back. A folder share covers every template in it, a folder's owner edits the templates in it, and a person's access is the highest of their template and folder shares. Owners and admins reach every template already, and viewers and agent tokens have no templates (403), so neither is shared with. Only a template's maker, owners and admins delete, share or move it, and only into a folder they own or may edit; anyone who may edit a folder saves new templates into it; people it is shared with cannot share it on. Each share records who granted it and when; the account audit log of grants is `ADMIN-005`'s. A template has up to 10 tags of 1 to 40 characters, set by whoever edits it. Search on the Templates screen and `GET /api/inkwell/templates?q=` keeps the templates whose name or a tag contains the text, ignoring case, among only those the person may use, and `folder=` keeps one folder (`personal` for their own); a template a person may not use is not found by search, list, document, send or match (404). Group shares join folders and templates through `ADMIN-002`'s groups before they ship. **Acceptance evidence:** a member sees and uses a shared template but cannot edit it; an editor changes it; a member without access cannot find it through the UI or API.

- **TMPL-003 — Template versions that never change sent envelopes.** Editing a template creates a new version; envelopes already sent keep the version they were sent from, and the record names it. A sent envelope can be saved as a new template. An edit, from the template's own edit screen, replaces its PDF, roles, fields and settings and raises its version by one (a new template is version 1); the template keeps only its latest version, and each earlier version lives on in the envelopes sent from it, which hold their own bytes, fields and settings, so no edit reaches them. The envelope and its prepared event name the template's ID, name and version. Its maker, owners, admins and people it is shared with to edit (`TMPL-002`) edit it; a share to use does not. Anyone who makes templates saves a native envelope they may read as a new version-1 template from its detail page: its original PDF; its recipients still on it, as roles in the same routing order with their access-code setting and private message, named as in the template the envelope came from or otherwise Signer 1, Signer 2 and so on; their fields; and its subject, message, reminders, options and expiry length in whole days (1 to 365). **Acceptance evidence:** send from version 1, edit the template, send again; the first envelope still shows version 1 fields and the second version 2; saving the first as a template reproduces its fields and roles.

- **TMPL-004 — Several templates in one envelope.** A sender applies two or more templates to one envelope in a chosen order, alongside any ad hoc documents (`PREP-002`). Recipients whose role name and routing order match across templates merge into one recipient, and a preview shows the merged recipient list before sending. When templates disagree (a role at different orders, different identity methods, reminders or expiry), the preview names the conflict and the sender chooses; without a choice, envelope-level settings follow the first template and identity follows the stricter method. Each template's fields stay on its own documents. The record and certificate name every template and version used. **Acceptance evidence:** an NDA template (role Client, order 1) and a W-9 template (role Client, order 1) go out as one envelope; the client signs both in one session and the certificate lists both template versions; a third template whose Client role sits at order 2 is shown as a separate recipient in the preview before sending; when the W-9 template requires an SMS code and the NDA an email code, the preview names the conflict and proposes the SMS code.

- **TMPL-005 — Template matching on upload.** When a sender uploads a document that matches a template's document (by fingerprint or by page-text similarity), Inkwell offers that template's fields and roles, with the match confidence; the sender applies or ignores the offer, and nothing is placed without their choice. Exact semantics: an upload on the prepare screen is compared with the document of every template the person may use (`TMPL-001`, including those shared with them, `TMPL-002`). The same file is its SHA-256 fingerprint, confidence 100%. Otherwise, when the upload has the same number of pages as the template's document at the same sizes (within one point, so every field lands where the template put it), the two are compared by page text: the words their text operators show (letters and digits, case ignored, one byte per character), as the share of distinct three-word runs they have in common (Jaccard), offered from 80% and shown rounded down to a whole percent; each document needs at least 20 words, so a scan with no text matches only by fingerprint. Up to five matches are offered, best first. Applying one sets one recipient per template role, in its routing order with its private message and access-code requirement (the send asks for that person's code), keeps any name and email already typed in that place, and places every template field where the template put it; the envelope's other settings stay the sender's, and it is an ad hoc envelope, not a send from the template (`TMPL-001`'s record). Matching places and stores nothing. Later upload formats (`PREP-001`'s Word and images) and `PREP-002`'s added documents are matched before they ship. **Acceptance evidence:** upload a fresh copy of the W-9 a template uses; the match is offered and applying it places every template field; an unrelated agreement is offered nothing.

### Recipients and routing

- **ROUTE-001 — Any number of recipients and roles.** An envelope has up to 100 recipients, each with a role: signer, approver (approves without signing), form filler, witness (nominated by the signer they witness, `ROUTE-010`), in-person signer, specify-recipients agent, editor, needs to view (`ROUTE-011`), or receives a copy. Copy recipients receive the completed record and no action link; each recipient sees only their own actions and only the documents visible to them (`ROUTE-012`). Each role added here carries `ROUTE-003`'s include rules (a signer's rules for roles that act, a copy recipient's for roles that only receive) before it ships. **Acceptance evidence:** send to eight signers, one approver, one form filler and three copy recipients, where signer 1 must have a witness; each sees only their own actions; signer 1 nominates a witness who is invited only after signer 1 signs and before order 2; copy recipients receive only the completed record.

- **ROUTE-002 — Sequential, parallel and mixed order.** Recipients share or differ in routing order: equal numbers act in parallel, higher numbers wait for all lower ones to finish. A routing order is a whole number from 1 to 100 set per recipient, in any list position and with gaps allowed; a recipient given none takes their list position (1 for the first). Whoever is unsigned at the lowest open order is invited together, each with their own link and authentication, and the next order is invited once every one of them has signed (electronically or by an accepted paper scan, `SIGNX-008`); a copy keeps each recipient's order (`PREP-010`). An envelope saved before routing orders existed signs one recipient at a time in list order. **Acceptance evidence:** recipients at orders 1, 2, 2 and 3; both order-2 recipients are invited together after order 1, and order 3 is invited only after both finish.

- **ROUTE-003 — Conditional routing.** A routing rule includes or skips a recipient based on a field value (for example, amounts over 10,000 USD add the finance director). Skipped recipients are recorded with the rule that skipped them. A rule is one field compared by equals or does-not-equal, or, for a USD number field, by greater than, less than, at least or at most, using `PREP-004`'s exact cents (the comparison value is kept to two decimals; a checkbox compares with true or false). A signer's rule reads only immutable sources: a locked `PREP-008` prefill, decided at approval, or a field completed by a signer at a lower routing order, decided in the same commit as that signature. A locked source that is missing or hidden refuses the approval; a lower-order answer that resolves to nothing stops the route with an explicit unresolved condition until the sender corrects it (`DELIV-006`), never a silent include or skip. A skipped signer is not a party: no invitation, code, action, completed-copy mail or link (`DELIV-007`); their placed fields neither print nor need completing; the skip (field, comparison, value, result with the source value, and time) is in the audit trail and on the certificate the parties receive. A copy recipient may carry the same one-field rule, evaluated once at completion against the final values (locked prefills and every completed field): false means no completed-copy mail or link, recorded on the certificate like a skipped signer; a source still missing or hidden at completion sends the copy, since a copy carries no signing authority, and the certificate records "rule unresolved, copy sent". With mail not set up no copy is sent, and the certificate says no completed-copy email was queued. Before sending, the template, send and approval screens show a routing preview of who is included or skipped on the current locked values and who still waits on a signer's answer. Copies of an envelope and templates made from or sent with it keep the rules and reset the outcomes. A signer's rule changes only by an audited `DELIV-006` correction while that signer's turn is not yet activated and they have neither signed nor been skipped; no correction revives a skipped recipient or changes a field already used to decide. The recipient roles `ROUTE-001` and `ROUTE-011` add carry these rules before they ship. **Acceptance evidence:** two envelopes from one template with amounts of 5,000 and 25,000 USD; only the second routes to the finance director, and the first's certificate names the skipped step and its rule; a copy recipient whose rule is an amount over 10,000 USD gets the completed copy only from the 25,000 envelope, the 5,000 certificate names the skipped copy and its rule, and a copy whose source is missing at completion is sent and recorded as "rule unresolved, copy sent"; the routing preview shows the skip before sending.

- **ROUTE-004 — Internal approval before sending.** A template or account rule can require a named approver to approve an envelope before it goes to external recipients, with approve and reject (with reason) actions and an audit entry for each. The exact semantics: the account rule is set or cleared by an owner or admin and kept with who set it and when; a template's rule is saved with the template's version by whoever may edit the template, and every envelope prepared from it carries it through draft edits, copies (`PREP-010`) and templates saved from it. A rule names one approver, an active owner, admin or sender (never a viewer), and optionally a threshold: a USD number field's label (ignoring case and surrounding spaces) and an amount. A rule without a threshold covers every envelope; one with a threshold covers an envelope with a number field of that label whose value is a locked sender value (`PREP-008`) strictly above the amount, or is not a locked sender value (its amount is not known before sending); an envelope without such a field is not covered. The account rule and the envelope's template rule both apply, and each distinct approver they name must approve. The person whose eligibility confirmation authorizes the send (`SEND-001`) remains its authority; while any named approver has not approved, the draft waits, locked against edits and a second approval, with no invitations, links or in-app signing, and it can still be voided or expire. Each approver is notified (`DELIV-008`, an 'Internal approval' notice by email unless they turn it off) and finds it in their waiting-for-me list (`MANAGE-003`). The last approval sends it exactly as the confirming person's approval would have (a scheduled time that has passed sends at once); one rejection, with a reason of 3 to 500 characters, returns it to its sender as an editable draft with the reason shown and notified, and approving it again makes a new request. When the confirming person is a named approver, their confirmation is that approval and is recorded so. A pending request keeps the rules as they were when it was made; each approver's role is checked again when they decide. Anyone a request named may read the envelope from then on, including after deciding, but never edit or share it. The request, each approval and each rejection are audit events naming their actor, and the send's approval event lists the approvals. `ADMIN-004`'s sending policies require an internal approval through these rules, as `SEND-001` states. **Acceptance evidence:** a sender's envelope above the rule's threshold waits for the approver; a rejection returns it to the sender with the reason; an approval sends it.

- **ROUTE-005 — Signing groups.** A recipient can be a signing group; any one member signs for it, and the record names who did. The exact semantics: owners and admins keep named groups in Settings (a name unique ignoring case, 2 to 10 members, each a name and a distinct email, workspace members or not); a sender chooses a group instead of a person for a recipient place, and the draft snapshots the group's name and members each time it is saved, so a later change to the group never changes a prepared or sent envelope (a copy, `PREP-010`, keeps its source's snapshot until its draft is saved again). The group takes one routing place (`ROUTE-002`) and counts as one of the envelope's signers; nobody may sign both alone and in a group on one envelope. At its turn every member gets their own link and verifies as the place's method requires (an emailed code, or the link alone under `IDV-001`); access codes, in-person signing and reassignment are refused for groups in v1, and so is a group while mail is not set up, because each member's link and code go by email. The first member to sign (electronically, or with a paper scan the sender accepts, `SIGNX-008`) completes the place; the other members' links then show "Signed for <group> by <member> at <time>" and can no longer sign, open the document or download the completed files. A member's decline or withdrawal of consent declines the envelope, naming that member. Reminders (`DELIV-003`) and resends (`DELIV-005`) go to every member while the place is open; the completed copy (`DELIV-007`) goes to the member who signed, not the others. A correction (`DELIV-006`) keeps an unsigned group place as it is or removes it; it never changes a group's members and cannot add a group. The certificate names the group, the member who signed or declined with that member's authentication evidence only, and every member invited. `ROUTE-003` include rules apply to a group place as to a person. A template role may be filled by a group when sending (`TMPL-001`), and `MOVE-002` imports Docusign signing groups as these groups before it ships. **Acceptance evidence:** send to the "Company Signers" group of three; one member signs, the others' links show it is done, and the certificate names the signer and the group.

- **ROUTE-006 — Delegation and reassignment.** When the sender allows it, a recipient reassigns their action to someone else with a reason; the sender can also replace an unsigned recipient (a `DELIV-006` correction). The original recipient's link stops working and the record shows both people. Exact semantics: the sender turns reassignment on per envelope when preparing it, and it is off otherwise (the account default joins `ADMIN-004`); a recipient reassigns from their signing page while it is their turn, after verifying, like every other recipient action; the new person takes the same place, routing order, fields and private message, and the same access code when the sender set one (the sender shares it with them); they are invited at once on their own link and verify their own email, and nothing the original recipient verified or agreed to carries over; the new email may not already belong to a recipient or copy recipient of the envelope; reassignment needs mail set up, since the new person can only be reached by email; the audit trail, the detail page's timeline and the certificate name both people, the time and the reason, and the person who now holds the place may reassign again while it is their turn. **Acceptance evidence:** a signer reassigns to a colleague; the colleague verifies and signs; the original link refuses; a disallowed reassignment is refused.

- **ROUTE-007 — Hosted in-person signing.** A team member hosts an in-person signer on their own device or a tablet; the signer’s identity is attested by the host, and the record names both. The sender marks the recipient in person when preparing; their name and email remain required for the record and completed copy, but no invitation, reminder or resend is emailed to them. At their routing turn, a member who may edit the envelope (`ADMIN-001`) attests “I confirmed <name>’s identity in person” by government photo ID or personally known to me; no ID image or number is kept. The host must be a different person: neither their email nor user may match the signer. The handoff revokes the host’s app session and opens the ordinary signer page in a session scoped to that one envelope and signer, for up to 24 hours. The hosted device can read only that signing request, cannot navigate into `/app` or read other app data, and receives no saved host signature. The signer agrees to a separately versioned hosted disclosure that names the sender and their email for direct paper-copy requests, withdrawal and contact changes (with the host able to help them reach the sender); its exact displayed words are fingerprinted, and the remote disclosure is unchanged. The signer signs themselves; signing, declining or withdrawing consent ends the hosted session and returns the device to the host’s sign-in. A correction keeps in-person versus remote mode after approval (changing that mode requires a new prepared envelope and approval); a corrected attendee needs a fresh host attestation. An in-person signer cannot reassign remotely; the sender may correct who attends. The audit trail records the host, their sign-in method, attestation method and time, IP, user agent and browser-reported screen size. The certificate reads “Signed in person, hosted by <host>; identity attested by the host, not verified by Inkwell”, with the attestation method and time and device information labeled “as reported by the browser”. **Acceptance evidence:** an admin hosts an in-person signer at 768 pixels, the signer signs, and the certificate records the host, the attestation and the device; the same preparation, attestation, signing and return to sign-in work at 1440 and 390 pixels, with direct transport assertions of the identity and session boundaries.

- **ROUTE-008 — Contacts and recent recipients.** Senders pick recipients from a shared contact book and recent recipients, with name, email, phone and company; contacts can be imported from CSV and synced from the CRM. **Acceptance evidence:** import 200 contacts from CSV and autocomplete a recipient by company. The book is one per account: owners, admins and senders read, add, change, remove and import contacts, and viewers and agent tokens cannot (`ACCESS-001`). A contact is one email, kept in lower case, with a name (up to 160 characters) and an optional phone (digits, spaces, brackets, dots or dashes with an optional leading +, up to 40 characters) and company (up to 160); saving or importing an email already in the book updates that contact. A CSV import is UTF-8 text up to 1 MiB whose first row names the columns, name and email and optionally phone and company in any order (other columns are ignored), then up to 1,000 rows; each invalid row is reported with its line number and nothing of it is saved. While preparing, the sender searches the book by name, email or company (case-insensitive, contains) and adds a contact as a signer or as a copy recipient; with nothing typed they see their recent recipients, the last 20 different signers and copy recipients on envelopes they prepared, newest first, with the book's company. Contacts are installation state and do not travel in an archive. Syncing from the CRM arrives with the CRM connectors: `INTEG-001`, `INTEG-002` and `INTEG-003` each bring their contacts into this book before they ship.

- **ROUTE-009 — Sign it yourself.** A signed-in member signs their own document with nobody else involved: they upload it (or pick it from a connected store or mailbox add-in, `INTEG-004`, `INTEG-005`), place their own fields, and sign in the app with no invitation and no mailbox code, because their signed-in session (and its sign-in method: email link, passkey or SSO, with any second factor under `ADMIN-009`) is the authentication, which the certificate records. They then download the executed PDF and certificate, file them, and optionally send the completed copy to other people. A member's adopted signature and initials are saved to their profile and reused for later self-signing and in-app signing (`MANAGE-003`) until they change them. When the sender is also a recipient at the first routing order of their own envelope, they sign in the app immediately after approving it, with no email to themselves, and only then is the next order invited. **Acceptance evidence:** a member self-signs a three-page PDF and the mail fake records no message; the certificate names session authentication and the sign-in method; the member downloads the executed PDF and certificate and sends the completed copy to two addresses; a second self-signed document reuses the saved signature without adopting it again; on another envelope the sender at order 1 signs in the app right after approval, and the order-2 signer is invited only after that signature. Here the sender is the member who approves: every first-order recipient whose email is theirs is left out of the invitations and signs in the app, so a self-signed envelope (approved, then signed by its only recipient) sends no message, and the envelope's own sender is never emailed its completed copy because their account files it (`DELIV-007`). The certificate states the session's sign-in method as Inkwell records it: an emailed invitation link, an emailed sign-in link, owner setup, single sign-on (OIDC), a password, local development sign-in or review sign-in; passkeys and second factors are added to it by `ADMIN-009` before it ships. The saved signature is the typed name a member last signed with through their session; it is offered for their next in-app signing, and signing with another replaces it; the record and certificate say when it was reused. Saved initials are offered with `PREP-003` round A. Picking a document from a store or mailbox arrives with `INTEG-004` and `INTEG-005`, each before it ships. The completed copy goes to 1–20 more people, within the envelope's 20 copy recipients, by whoever may change the envelope, through `DELIV-007`'s delivery, and the record names who sent it. Saved signatures are installation state and do not travel in an archive.

- **ROUTE-010 — Witnesses nominated by their signer.** The sender requires or allows up to two witnesses for a signer. The signer nominates each witness by name and email while signing (or the sender names them in advance), and a witness cannot be the signer or another recipient of the envelope. A witness is invited only after their signer signs and before any later routing order proceeds, authenticates with the method the sender set (`IDV-001`), sees only the documents their signer signed with that signer's completed signature and their own witness fields (signature, printed name, date and, when set, address), and signs. If a witness declines, the step returns to the signer to nominate someone else. The certificate links each witness to the signer they witnessed, with both parties' authentication evidence. **Acceptance evidence:** signer 1 nominates a witness while signing; the witness is invited only after signer 1 signs and order 2 waits for the witness; the witness verifies, sees only signer 1's document and their own fields, not a document visible only to signer 2, and signs; the certificate names the witness against signer 1; nominating the signer's own email as witness is refused; a declining witness returns the step to signer 1, who nominates another.

- **ROUTE-011 — Recipients who name, edit or only view.** Besides signers and approvers, a recipient can be a specify-recipients agent, who enters the names and emails of later recipients the sender left open; an editor, who may change later recipients, their fields and the messages before passing the envelope on, with every change audited; needs to view, who must open the documents before routing continues; or certified delivery, who must open them and whose opening is recorded as proof of delivery. Each of these roles carries `ROUTE-003`'s include rules, as a recipient who acts, before it ships. **Acceptance evidence:** an agent at order 1 names the order-3 signer, who is then invited; an editor at order 2 adds a field and changes a later recipient's email, and the audit trail shows both; a needs-to-view recipient blocks order 3 until they open the documents; a certified-delivery recipient's view time is on the certificate.

- **ROUTE-012 — Document visibility per recipient.** In a multi-document envelope the sender chooses which documents each recipient may see; by default a recipient sees every document, and an account policy can hide documents in which a recipient has no fields. Hidden documents are absent from that recipient's signing view, downloads, completed copy, embedded view and API recipient view, and the certificate states which documents each recipient was shown. **Acceptance evidence:** an envelope with an offer letter and a compensation memo for the hiring manager: the candidate never sees the memo while signing, in downloads, in their completed copy or through the API recipient view; the manager sees and signs both; the certificate lists each recipient's visible documents.

### Signer experience

- **SIGNX-001 — Guided mobile-first signing.** Signing works on a phone at 360 pixels without zooming: a Start and Next button walks the signer through every required field, a progress count shows what remains, the document pages render sharply and load progressively, and the signer can finish later from the same link. **Acceptance evidence:** a signer completes a ten-field, five-page envelope at 390 pixels using only Next, leaves halfway, returns and finishes; the journey records time to first page under 3 seconds on a throttled mobile profile. Start, then each Next, goes to the next of the signer's required fields still empty in reading order (by page, then top to bottom, then left to right), scrolls the document to it and puts its entry at the foot of the screen; after the last, Next goes to the consent. Every field placed today is required; optional fields (`PREP-003`) and fields a signer places themselves (`PREP-011`) join Next and the count before they ship. The count reads how many of the signer's required fields are complete and how many are left. Each Next, and Finish later, saves the signer's entries on the server; reopening the same link (after the same verification as signing: the 24-hour signing session, or a new code) brings them back, and Start resumes at the first empty field. Saved entries sign nothing, are not evidence and are not archived; they are cleared when the signer signs, electronically or on paper, or the envelope is declined or voided. Pages appear as each one loads and are drawn only near the screen, at the screen's pixel density up to 3x. Time to first page runs from opening the signing link to the first page drawn; the throttled mobile profile is a 390 by 844 pixel screen at 3x density with touch, the CPU slowed 4x and the network held to 150 ms round trips, 9 Mbit/s down and 1.5 Mbit/s up (Chrome DevTools Protocol), on a cold HTTP cache.

- **SIGNX-002 — Draw, type or upload signatures and initials.** The signer adopts a signature and initials by typing (choice of styles), drawing with finger or mouse, or uploading an image; the adopted marks are applied to every signature and initials field, and the record keeps the adoption method. A signed-in member's adopted marks are saved and reused (`ROUTE-009`). **Acceptance evidence:** one signer draws on a touch screen, another types and a third uploads; all three appear in the executed PDF and the certificate records each method. The signer enters their full name in each signature field (the same name in all of them) and adopts one mark: the name typed in a style (Sans italic, Serif italic or Serif bold italic, set in the PDF standard fonts), a drawing made on the signing page with a finger, pen or mouse (kept as a PNG cropped to the ink), or an uploaded PNG or JPEG of at most 512 KiB (524,288 bytes). The executed PDF carries that mark in every signature field: a typed name in its style, or the image centered in the field and scaled to fit with its proportions kept. A signer who adopts nothing is typed in Sans italic, as every envelope signed before adoption existed was. The mark is adopted at signing: Finish later (`SIGNX-001`) keeps the names entered but not the adopted mark. The record keeps the method, the typed style or the image's type and SHA-256, and the image itself with the envelope's documents (so it travels in archives and is erased with the envelope); the certificate states each signature's method with its style or fingerprint (`RECORD-002`). A member's saved mark keeps its method and image and is offered on their next in-app signing. `PREP-003` round A adds initials adoption in the same three ways and applies the independent initials mark to every initials field.

- **SIGNX-003 — Accessible signing.** Signing and preparation meet WCAG 2.2 AA: keyboard-only completion, screen-reader labels for every field and its state, visible focus, 200% zoom without loss, and sufficient contrast in both themes. **Acceptance evidence:** an automated accessibility scan with no serious violations on the signing and preparation pages, plus a keyboard-only and a screen-reader journey that complete an envelope. The scan is axe-core's WCAG 2.0, 2.1 and 2.2 A and AA rules (contrast included), run on the preparation page and on each signing step (code, disclosure, fields) in the light and dark themes at 1440 and 390 pixels, and passes with no violation rated serious or critical. Keyboard-only means Tab, Enter, Space, letter keys and typing: the owner prepares (title, PDF through the file chooser, recipient, a field placed on each of two pages with Enter, field type chosen by keyboard), approves and the signer verifies, agrees, signs through Start and Next and signs, and every control reached shows a focus outline or ring (a date field's calendar button shows the browser's own). The screen-reader journey reads the browser's accessibility tree, the same one screen readers use, with no operating-system screen reader: each of the signer's fields is named with its label, page and type and is required, its entered value is exposed, the progress count is a live status, the adopted signature style and the consent expose their checked state, and the signer completes the envelope by roles and names alone. 200% zoom is a 1440 by 900 screen at 200%, 720 by 450 CSS pixels at 2x density: preparation and signing show without sideways scrolling, as they do at 390 and 360 pixels (`SIGNX-001`). Beside the document, the signer's fields panel scrolls within the space the guide leaves, so the guide never covers the consent or Sign document. Screens that later clauses add to signing or preparation (`PREP-003`'s field types, `PREP-005`'s drag and resize, `SIGNX-007`'s Spanish pages, `SIGNX-009`'s comments) join this scan and these journeys before they ship.

- **SIGNX-004 — Decline with reason.** A signer declines with a required reason; the envelope stops, the sender and earlier signers are notified, and the reason is in the record. **Acceptance evidence:** the second signer declines; the third is never invited, the sender receives a notice with the reason, and the certificate records the decline.

- **SIGNX-005 — ESIGN and UETA consumer disclosure.** Before first signing, a signer reviews the account's electronic record and signature disclosure (how to withdraw consent, request a paper copy, update contact details and the hardware and software needed) and agrees to it; the disclosure version and agreement time are in the record. A signer can withdraw consent, which stops the envelope and notifies the sender. **Acceptance evidence:** a new signer sees and accepts the disclosure; a second signer withdraws consent and the envelope stops with the withdrawal recorded; the certificate quotes the disclosure version.

- **SIGNX-006 — Branded sending and signing.** An account brand sets logo, colors, sender display name, reply-to address, email footer and a custom sending domain; brands can differ per template. **Acceptance evidence:** two brands on two templates; invitations and signing pages show each brand's logo and colors, and email headers use its reply-to and domain.

- **SIGNX-007 — English and Spanish signing.** Signing pages, emails, disclosures and the certificate are available in English and Spanish, chosen per recipient or by the signer. **Acceptance evidence:** a recipient set to Spanish receives a Spanish invitation, signs on Spanish pages and receives a Spanish completion email; the certificate shows the language used. The Spanish signing pages join `SIGNX-003`'s accessibility scan and its keyboard-only and screen-reader journeys before they ship.

- **SIGNX-008 — Signer document actions and paper signing.** Before and after signing, a signer can download and print the documents visible to them (`ROUTE-012`). When the sender allows it, a signer can choose to sign on paper instead: they download the documents, sign them by hand, and upload a scan or phone photo; the sender reviews and accepts or rejects the upload, and the record marks that recipient's signature as wet ink with the uploaded pages' fingerprint, which the certificate states. **Acceptance evidence:** a signer downloads and prints the document before signing; on an envelope that allows paper signing, a signer uploads a three-page scan, the sender accepts it and the certificate marks the signature as wet ink with the scan's fingerprint; when paper signing is not allowed, the option is absent and the API refuses the upload.

- **SIGNX-009 — Recipient comments and supplemental documents.** Recipients and the sender exchange comments on a document while it is out for signature, threaded and anchored to a page, visible to the parties the author chooses and kept in the record. The sender attaches supplemental documents (terms, policies, a handbook) that recipients must view or acknowledge but do not sign; each acknowledgment is recorded. **Acceptance evidence:** a signer comments on page 2, the sender replies, and the next signer sees the thread when it was shared with them; a supplemental handbook must be acknowledged before the signer can finish, and the certificate lists the acknowledgment and its time. Comments and supplemental documents join `SIGNX-003`'s accessibility scan and its keyboard-only and screen-reader journeys before they ship.

### Signer identity

- **IDV-001 — Per-recipient authentication choice.** For each recipient the sender chooses none (link only, as the incumbents default to), email code, access code, SMS code, knowledge-based or phone authentication (`IDV-005`) or identity verification, with account defaults (`ADMIN-004`) and template settings; the first-release email code (`SIGN-001`) is one of these methods, not a requirement on every recipient. The certificate records the method and outcome. Exact semantics today: on the prepare screen, a draft, a correction and each template role, the sender picks Email code (the default) or Link only for each recipient, and independently an access code (`IDV-003`), giving four methods: link only, email code, access code with the link alone, and access code with email code. A link-only recipient opens a 24-hour signing session from their link alone (after their access code, if any) with Continue; no code is sent or asked, and the opening is recorded with the client's network evidence. The sender is told "Anyone with this link can sign as" that recipient. Hosted in-person recipients instead use the host attestation and isolated session of `ROUTE-007`, selected on preparation; no email or link-only verification replaces the host’s attestation. Link only is refused while mail is not set up, where the owner's once-visible link would be the only factor. Changing a recipient's method in a correction is a new link (`DELIV-006`). The certificate's authentication line names each recipient's method and outcome ("link only, no code … opened", "emailed verification code, verified", "Access code: entered at"), as does the envelope timeline (`MANAGE-005`). SMS codes (`IDV-002`), knowledge-based and phone authentication (`IDV-005`) and identity verification (`IDV-004`) join this choice before they ship, and the account default is `ADMIN-004`'s. **Acceptance evidence:** one envelope with four recipients using four different methods, one of them link only; each must pass their own method, the link-only recipient signs with no code, and the certificate lists each method and result.

- **IDV-002 — SMS one-time codes.** A recipient verifies with a code sent by SMS to a US mobile number through the buyer's Twilio account or Inkwell's registered sender, with resend spacing, expiry and attempt limits matching email codes. The recipient's number is corrected in flight like their email (`DELIV-006`), and the sender resends the invitation by SMS like by email (`DELIV-005`). It joins `IDV-001`'s per-recipient choice (prepare screen, drafts, corrections, template roles) and the certificate's method and outcome line before it ships. **Acceptance evidence:** through an SMS provider fake, a recipient receives and enters a code, a wrong code and an expired code are refused, and the certificate records the masked number; a `DELIV-006` correction of a recipient's number is audited with its before and after values and the old link refuses; a `DELIV-005` resend by SMS through the provider fake reaches the signer, who opens the SMS link and signs, and after rotation the old link refuses.

- **IDV-003 — Access codes.** The sender sets an access code shared out of band; the recipient must enter it before seeing the document, with attempt limits. **Acceptance evidence:** a recipient is refused with a wrong code, locked after five attempts, and admitted with the right one.

- **IDV-004 — Identity document verification through a provider.** A recipient verifies a government photo ID and a selfie through Persona or Stripe Identity before signing; Inkwell stores only the provider's verification reference, result and name match, never the ID images. It joins `IDV-001`'s per-recipient choice (prepare screen, drafts, corrections, template roles) and the certificate's method and outcome line before it ships. **Acceptance evidence:** through a provider fake, a matching verification admits the signer, a failed one refuses with a retry, and the record holds only the reference and outcome.

- **IDV-005 — Knowledge-based and phone authentication.** For US recipients the sender can require knowledge-based authentication (questions from public and credit records through an identity provider such as LexisNexis Risk Solutions) or phone authentication (a code delivered by voice call or SMS through Twilio to a number the sender sets). Attempts are limited, a failed recipient is locked with a notice to the sender, and Inkwell stores only the provider's reference and pass or fail, never the questions or answers. It joins `IDV-001`'s per-recipient choice (prepare screen, drafts, corrections, template roles) and the certificate's method and outcome line before it ships. **Acceptance evidence:** through provider fakes, a recipient who passes the questions is admitted, one who fails twice is locked and the sender notified; a voice-call code admits a second recipient; the record holds only the references and outcomes.

### Delivery and follow-up

- **DELIV-001 — Live delivery with per-message status.** Invitations, codes and notices go out through the configured provider and each message shows queued, sent, delivered, opened, bounced or failed from provider events. Uncertain provider responses are never blindly retried. Today's providers are Resend, SendGrid and Cloudflare Email; adding Amazon SES, Postmark or SMTP sending belongs to `ADMIN-007`'s provider connections, and each inherits this clause before it ships: per-message status only from that provider's verified events, or this clause's no-confirmation label. Every message records the provider that accepted it and the message id it returned. Resend and SendGrid post signed delivery webhooks to `POST /api/inkwell/mail/events`, verified by Base's delivery-event intake with the installation's `MAIL_WEBHOOK_KEY` (Resend's `whsec_` signing secret, or SendGrid's public verification key); every event in a batch must name a message this installation sent to that recipient through that provider (Resend by its returned id, SendGrid by its signed delivery id), or the whole batch is refused and nothing is stored, and a replayed event changes nothing. Status follows Base's per-recipient delivery state: queued until the provider is called; sent once it accepts (a provider deferral stays sent, labeled delayed); delivered at the provider's delivered time, meaning the recipient's mail server accepted it, not that a person read it; opened at the earliest open the provider reports (Resend with open tracking on; Base's SendGrid sender turns open tracking off, so SendGrid messages stop at delivered); bounced; failed when the provider refuses it at send or later reports failure or suppression; a spam complaint shows as delivered, then marked as spam; uncertain when the provider's answer was unclear, and that message is never sent again automatically. Times are the provider's event times, stored in UTC. Labels are honest: Cloudflare Email, as Inkwell sends it, gives no delivery events, so its messages read "sent, no delivery confirmation from this provider" and are never shown as delivered, opened or bounced; a Resend or SendGrid installation without a webhook key reads "sent, delivery events are not set up"; the development outbox reads that it reached no mailbox. Bounce handling (flag, pause, alert, correct) is `DELIV-002`'s. **Acceptance evidence:** through a mail provider fake, a sent invitation moves to delivered and opened from provider webhooks, and an uncertain send is shown as uncertain with no duplicate.

- **DELIV-002 — Bounces and bad addresses.** A hard bounce or rejected address flags the recipient, pauses their reminders and alerts the sender, who corrects the address and resends in one step. Exact semantics: a recipient whose turn it is is flagged while their latest invitation is bounced or failed by `DELIV-001`'s status (the provider reported a bounce, a failure or a suppressed address, or refused the address at send); a provider that reports no delivery events (Cloudflare Email) never flags anyone. The flag shows on the envelope detail page (`MANAGE-005`); no automatic or manual reminder reaches a flagged recipient; the envelope's sender (the member who prepared it) is emailed once per bounced invitation, from the delivery webhook or, if that alert did not complete, from the next hourly pass. Whoever may change the envelope (`ADMIN-001`) enters a different address (and optionally name) not used by another recipient; the change is audited with its before and after values, the old link, code and session stop working, and the new invitation goes out in the same step. Reminders then resume on the envelope's schedule (`DELIV-003`). Other corrections are `DELIV-006`'s, and the in-app activity feed is `DELIV-008`'s. **Acceptance evidence:** a bounce event marks the recipient, the sender is alerted, corrects the email and the corrected recipient signs.

- **DELIV-003 — Automatic reminders.** Reminders start N days after a recipient's invitation and repeat every M days, only to recipients whose action is current, within the account's US time zone and business days, and stop at signature, decline, void or expiry. Account, template and envelope levels set them. N and M are whole business days from 1 to 30; business days are Monday to Friday in the account's time zone, which is America/New_York until the account setting of `ADMIN-004` lets the owner choose another US zone, and a due reminder goes out on the first hourly pass at or after 09:00 local time. A recipient's invitation is when the envelope was approved (first routing order) or when the last recipient at a lower order signed (`ROUTE-002`); later reminders count from the previous one. A recipient whose paper scan awaits review is not reminded (`SIGNX-008`). The reminder points to the recipient's invitation link; when mail is not set up no reminder is sent or recorded. Today the envelope and template levels ship: set when preparing, kept by a copy (`PREP-010`), and set by the template an envelope is sent from (`TMPL-001`); the account level arrives with `ADMIN-004`, which must set it before it ships. **Acceptance evidence:** with the clock advanced, a current signer receives reminders on days 2, 4 and 6 skipping a weekend, a waiting signer receives none, and reminders stop after the signature.

- **DELIV-004 — Configurable expiry with warnings.** Expiry is set per envelope, template or account (default 120 days), recipients and the sender are warned before it, and the sender can extend it before or after expiry without re-preparing. **Acceptance evidence:** an envelope set to expire in 10 days warns on day 7, the sender extends it by 14 days, and the signer signs on day 12.

- **DELIV-005 — Resend by email or SMS.** The sender resends the current invitation by email or SMS; the old link keeps working unless the sender rotates it. Resend is offered among the envelope detail page's actions (`MANAGE-005`), and each resent message shows its own status there (`DELIV-001`). Exact semantics: whoever may change the envelope (`ADMIN-001`) resends to a recipient whose turn it is, never to one whose invitation bounced (they correct the address, `DELIV-002`) or whose paper scan awaits review, and only with mail set up (without mail the owner creates a new private link, `SEND-001`); Resend emails the same link again, and Resend with a new link rotates it, so the previous link, code, session and access-code pass stop working; both are audited. To send the same link again, each recipient's current link is kept sealed with AES-GCM under a key derived from the installation's `SESSION_SECRET`, in a secret column that is never archived or exported and is cleared when the link rotates, the recipient signs or the envelope closes; a link issued before links were kept, or kept under another key, is rotated on resend and the sender is told so. A resend does not move the automatic reminder schedule (`DELIV-003`). Recipients have no phone number today: SMS resend arrives with `IDV-002`'s numbers, which carries it in its own text and acceptance. **Acceptance evidence:** resend by email through the provider fake: the same link again, then a new link, after which the old link refuses; the signer opens the resent link and signs; each resent message shows its own status; the resend is made from the envelope detail page (`MANAGE-005`).

- **DELIV-006 — Correct an envelope in flight.** Before a recipient acts, the sender (or anyone the envelope is shared with for editing, or an admin) changes their name, email or phone, adds or removes later recipients, and edits the messages, expiry or the fields of recipients who have not yet acted, without invalidating signatures already given. Corrections are the only way a sent envelope changes (`SEND-001`): each is audited with its before and after values, old links stop working, and a field already signed and a signature already given can never be changed. Correct is offered among the envelope detail page's actions (`MANAGE-005`), and the corrected recipient's timeline continues there. Exact semantics: a recipient has acted once they signed, declined or uploaded a paper scan awaiting review, and from then on their name, email, routing order, private message and fields are fixed; "later" recipients are those who have not signed, and each may sit at any routing order no lower than the highest order of a recipient who has signed; a removed recipient keeps their place in the record marked removed (their fields go with them) and is never invited, reminded or sent a copy; at least one recipient who has not signed always remains (to stop, void the envelope); a recipient whose name or email changes is a new person at that place, so nothing the previous person verified or agreed to carries over, and their old link, code, session and saved entries stop working (a change of order or private message alone keeps the link); whoever's turn it is after the correction and was corrected, added or newly reached is invited at once on a new link, and a changed subject or message applies to invitations sent from then on; corrections are open while the envelope awaits signature and has not expired (extend it first, `DELIV-004`). Recipients have no phone number today: `IDV-002` adds the number and correcting it here before it ships. **Acceptance evidence:** after signer 1 signs, correct signer 2's email and add a copy recipient; signer 1's signature stands, the old link for signer 2 refuses, and the new one completes the envelope; an attempt to change signer 1's signed field, through the correction screen or the API, is refused; the audit trail shows each correction with its before and after values.

- **DELIV-007 — Completed copies to every party.** On completion, every recipient and copy recipient receives the executed documents and certificate as an attachment or a secure link (account setting; attachments above 10 MB become links), and the sender's account files them. An envelope has up to 20 copy recipients today (`ROUTE-001`'s copy role, each email different from every signer's), who get no action link. The setting is the owner's or an admin's for the whole account (with `ADMIN-004`'s account defaults when they ship), a secure link until changed; with attachments chosen, an executed PDF and certificate together above 10,000,000 bytes go as a secure link. A secure link belongs to one recipient and opens only after a six-digit code sent to that recipient's mailbox (one a minute, ten per link, each valid 10 minutes with five tries), then stays open in that browser for 24 hours; it works while the envelope exists and stops at erasure (`ERASE-001`). Copy links and the setting are installation state and do not travel in an archive. The sender's account files the copies by keeping the executed PDF and certificate on the completed envelope, so the envelope's own sender, when also a recipient, is not emailed one (`ROUTE-009`). The record names every recipient queued and the delivery used, and each code request and check on a link; when mail is not set up no copy is sent or recorded as sent, and the envelope says so. Recipients that later clauses add (`ROUTE-001`, `ROUTE-011`) receive the completed copy this way before they ship, and `ROUTE-012` limits each copy to that recipient's visible documents. **Acceptance evidence:** complete an envelope with two signers and a copy recipient; all three receive the completed copy through the mail fake, and secure links require their mailbox code.

- **DELIV-008 — Sender notifications.** Senders get viewed, signed, declined, completed, bounced and expiring notices by email, in an in-app activity feed and, when connected, in Slack, Microsoft Teams or Nook; each person chooses which they receive. The exact semantics: a notice goes to the member who sent the envelope and every member holding an edit share of it (`ADMIN-001`), for these events: a recipient viewed the document, signed (also on paper), declined or withdrew consent, everyone signed, an invitation bounced (`DELIV-002`), or the envelope expires in three days (`DELIV-004`); every notice is in that member's activity feed, newest first; until a member chooses, email carries declined, completed, bounced and expiring notices and nothing else does; a member is never emailed or posted about their own action (`ROUTE-009`); with mail not set up no notice email is written. Slack and Microsoft Teams connect by an incoming-webhook URL the member pastes (https on hooks.slack.com, or a Teams or Workflows host under webhook.office.com, logic.azure.com or environment.api.powerplatform.com), Nook by its installation's Slack-compatible incoming webhook (https, path /hooks/slack/, `NOOK-063`); a URL is sealed at rest, shown only masked, never archived and erased on disconnect; a post names the envelope, the recipient and what happened, never a field value or the document's text (an email of a decline also gives its reason); a post is tried at most three times and the feed shows whether it was delivered. Nook's incoming webhooks are `NOOK-063` in run-nook: until it ships, the Nook leg is proven against a fake that answers as `NOOK-063` documents, and `NOOK-063` carries the Nook test installation passing this journey before it ships. **Acceptance evidence:** a sender subscribed to declines and completions in Slack, Microsoft Teams, Nook and email receives each through the Slack and Teams fakes, a Nook test installation and the mail fake, and nothing else; after unsubscribing from completions in Teams, the next completion reaches the other three channels and not Teams; every notice appears in the in-app activity feed.

A draft is prepared and awaiting approval; waiting for me is the signed-in person's own signing turn (`MANAGE-003`); waiting for others is every other envelope out for signature; an unfinished envelope past its expiry is expired; a person sees only the envelopes their role allows (`ACCESS-001`). - **MANAGE-003 — Waiting for me.** Team members who must sign or approve envelopes from colleagues see them in a waiting-for-me list and sign inside the app without an email round trip. Any member, whatever their role, signs their own turn this way, and owners and admins also find the prepared envelopes awaiting their approval there, and every named internal approver (`ROUTE-004`) finds the drafts waiting for their decision. The member's signed-in session replaces the emailed or sender-provided code: opening their turn issues a fresh signing link, already verified in that browser for 24 hours, that replaces the link they were sent, and the record and certificate name the member whose session authenticated the signature. - **MANAGE-004 — Reports and exports.** Reports show envelopes sent, completed, declined and expired, time to complete, and per sender and per template turnaround over a date range, with CSV export. A range is whole days in the account's US time zone (America/New_York until `ADMIN-004` lets the owner choose), both days included, at most 731 days. Sent counts approvals in the range; completed and declined count those outcomes in the range, a withdrawal of consent counting as declined (`SIGNX-005`); expired counts sent, unfinished envelopes whose expiry passed in the range. Time to complete runs from approval to completion, in hours, over the envelopes completed in the range: the median (the mean of the middle two for an even count) and the mean, each rounded to 0.1 hour. Rows group by the member who prepared the envelope; a sender reports only on their own envelopes (`ACCESS-001`), and imported records are not counted. The CSV holds one row per sender and an all-senders row with the same numbers, and a cell that would start with =, +, - or @ starts with an apostrophe instead. Per-template turnaround is added to the report and its CSV before `TMPL-001` ships. **Acceptance evidence:** a fixture of 300 envelopes produces the expected counts and median turnaround, and the CSV matches the report.

- **MANAGE-005 — Envelope detail and timeline.** Each envelope has a detail page showing its documents, every recipient's place in routing and timeline (sent, delivered, viewed, authenticated, signed, declined or bounced, with times in the account's US time zone and UTC), what each recipient is waiting on, and the actions available to the viewer (remind, resend, correct, void, copy, download), next to the audit trail. Exact semantics: the account's US time zone is America/New_York until `ADMIN-004` lets the owner choose another, and each time shows as, for example, "Oct 19, 2026, 10:02 AM EDT · 14:02 UTC". Sent is when the provider accepted the invitation (or, with mail not set up, when the owner created the private link); delivered and bounced are the provider's event times for that invitation (`DELIV-001`); viewed is the first time the recipient opened the unsigned document after verifying and agreeing to electronic records; authenticated is their first verification (emailed code, sender-provided code or signed-in session); signed and declined are the signer record's times. A step with no recorded fact is not shown. Waiting on names the sender's approval or the scheduled send, the earlier routing order and who in it, the recipient's verification, their agreement to electronic records, their signature, or the sender's review of a paper scan. Remind is offered to whoever may change the envelope (`ADMIN-001`) while it is open for signing with mail set up; it sends one reminder now to each recipient whose turn it is (never to one waiting for an earlier order or whose paper scan awaits review), recorded as a reminder so the automatic schedule (`DELIV-003`) counts from it. Resend (`DELIV-005`), correct (`DELIV-006`), void, copy and download are today's other actions, and the documents list grows with `PREP-002`; a recipient's timeline also shows each correction of their place (added, corrected with what changed, removed). **Acceptance evidence:** for an envelope with three recipients mid-routing, the detail page at 1440 and 390 pixels shows each recipient's sent, delivered, viewed and signed times matching the provider events (`DELIV-001`) and the audit trail, and its remind action sends one reminder to the current signer only.

### Execution evidence

- **RECORD-002 — Per-recipient certificate evidence.** The certificate of completion lists for every recipient their authentication method and result, IP address, user agent, adopted signature and method, disclosure acceptance, and every timestamp in UTC and the account's US time zone, plus every document's fingerprint. The IP address is the connecting address Cloudflare reports for the request and reads "not recorded" when there is none; the account's US time zone is America/New_York until `ADMIN-004` lets the owner choose another; the result of a code check includes how many incorrect codes are on record. It is done when every method that exists today conforms (emailed code, sender-provided code, a member's signed-in session under `MANAGE-003`, access codes, typed and paper signatures), and `IDV-002`, `IDV-004`, `IDV-005` and `SIGNX-002` add their own method, result and adoption lines before they ship. **Acceptance evidence:** complete an envelope with an email-code signer and a member who signs in the app (and SMS-code signers once `IDV-002` ships) and show each item on the certificate matches the audit trail.

- **RECORD-003 — Sealed PDFs.** The executed PDF carries a PDF digital signature (PAdES) from the installation's signing certificate or a certificate on the Adobe Approved Trust List obtained through a provider, so Adobe Acrobat shows the document as unmodified and any change breaks the seal. **Acceptance evidence:** open the executed PDF in a PDF validator to show a valid seal; modify one byte and show the seal fails.

- **RECORD-004 — Public verification.** Anyone with an executed PDF or certificate uploads it to the installation's verification page and learns whether it matches a completed envelope, without signing in and without revealing other envelopes. **Acceptance evidence:** a signed-out visitor verifies a genuine executed PDF, an altered copy fails, and an unrelated PDF reveals nothing.

- **RECORD-005 — Retention and legal hold.** Account and folder rules purge completed envelopes after a set number of years with notice to admins, and a legal hold stops purge and erasure for named envelopes. Exact semantics: the account rule is one whole number of years from 1 to 99, set or cleared by an owner or admin (`ADMIN-004`'s retention default), kept with who set it and when. It applies to every envelope that has ended: completed (at its completion), declined (at the decline or withdrawal of consent), voided (at the void) or expired (at its expiry, once that has passed without everyone signing); an envelope still open for signing is never purged. Imported records fall under the rule too: an imported envelope from its recorded terminal instant (the source's completion, decline or void time; where the source gave none, the import time, labeled "terminal time not in the source"), and a DocuSeal move-in archive when its latest envelope is due, with the owner's undo through the import door kept. An archive means all records sharing an uploaded source JSON (including overlapping imports); its due date is the latest terminal instant among those records plus the rule. A hold on any record keeps the connected archive and its shared source JSON; owner/admin hold and lift are audited, owner erase and import undo refuse while it is held, and an overdue lift restarts the warning. Existing DocuSeal jobs are backfilled from their recorded completion times without changing canonical imported values. Each future importer (`MOVE-002`, `MOVE-003`, `MOVE-004`, `MOVE-005`, `MOVE-006`, `MOVE-008`) must preserve a source terminal instant or durably record its import instant and the missing-source-time label before shipping. An envelope falls due at the instant it ended plus that many calendar years in UTC (a 29 February end falls due on 1 March); a warning stands only while that due date does, so an expired envelope the sender extends is no longer due. The hourly pass warns every active owner and admin once, in their activity feed (`DELIV-008`) and by email, naming the envelope, the rule and the purge date, 30 days before the envelope falls due, or at once when the rule is set or shortened past that point; it purges at the later of the due date and 30 days after that warning, and never without one. A purge is the owner's erasure: documents, audit events, recipients, messages and their provider delivery events, codes, copy links, shares, activity-feed notices and command results go, with Base's erasure record so restoring an earlier archive is refused naming the erasure; the retention ledger, which owners and admins read, keeps only the envelope ID, how it ended, the rule and the ended, warned and purged times. An owner or admin places a legal hold on a named envelope with a reason (3 to 500 characters) and lifts it; both are recorded in the envelope's audit trail. While an envelope is held the pass neither warns nor purges it and an erase request is refused naming the hold; lifting the hold restarts the 30-day warning. Folder rules arrive with `MANAGE-002`'s folders, which carry them (a folder's rule in place of the account rule for its envelopes, under these semantics) in their own text and acceptance before they ship. **Acceptance evidence:** with the clock advanced, a seven-year rule purges an envelope after its warning, and a held envelope survives both the rule and an erase request.

### Team administration

- **ADMIN-001 — Roles and envelope sharing.** Built-in roles are owner, admin, sender and viewer, and custom permission profiles refine them (`ADMIN-008`). Senders prepare, send, correct, remind and void their own envelopes and those shared with them for editing, and see those shared with them or their groups; admins see and act on all; viewers only read. Envelopes and folders can be shared with people and groups to view or to edit. These roles are the matrix every request is checked against (`ACCESS-001`). An envelope is shared today one member at a time, found by their email in this workspace, by its sender, an owner or an admin; sharing again changes the access and removing a share ends it at once; people it is shared with cannot share it on; a viewer-role member's edit share lets them read only, and applies if they become a sender; every grant and removal is an audited event on the envelope; shares are installation state, erased with the envelope and not carried in an archive. Folder shares arrive with `MANAGE-002`'s folders and group shares with `ADMIN-002`'s groups, each following these rules. **Acceptance evidence:** a sender sends, corrects and voids their own envelope; two senders cannot see each other's envelopes until one shares the envelope (or, once `MANAGE-002` ships folders, a folder), and a view-only share lets the other read but not void; a viewer can read but not send; the API enforces the same boundaries.

- **ADMIN-002 — Members, groups and departures.** Admins invite, deactivate and re-role members, manage groups, and transfer a departing member's envelopes and templates to someone else without breaking in-flight signing. - **ADMIN-003 — SSO and SCIM.** Members sign in through SAML or OIDC single sign-on (Okta, Microsoft Entra ID, Google Workspace, Auth) and are provisioned and deprovisioned through SCIM 2.0; SSO can be required for everyone but the owner's recovery path. **Acceptance evidence:** through IdP fakes, an OIDC and a SAML sign-in succeed, a SCIM create adds a member and a SCIM deactivate ends their sessions.

- **ADMIN-004 — Account defaults and sending policies.** Admins set default reminders, expiry, authentication methods (`IDV-001`), allowed signature adoption methods, branding, retention, the consumer disclosure, whether free-form signing is allowed (`PREP-011`), whether recipients may reassign their signing (`ROUTE-006`, today chosen per envelope) and sending policies. ## Push further

Agents carry the agreement from request to filed record. Each clause says what the agent does, on which data, and under what human control. Agents act through the same API and authority checks as people (`ACCESS-001`, `DELEGATE-001`), every action is in the audit trail with the agent named, and no agent agrees to terms, signs, sends or spends money without the grant the clause names.

### Agents that carry the agreement

- **AGENT-001 — Assemble the envelope from CRM and email context.** *What the agent does:* from a request ("send the MSA to Acme") or a deal stage change, picks the approved template, fills roles and fields from the CRM record and the email thread with the customer, and proposes signers with their authority (title, entity). *Data:* the CRM record (Orbit, HubSpot, Salesforce), the granted mailbox thread (Gmail, Outlook), templates and contacts. *Human control:* the sender sees a draft envelope with each value's source and confidence and approves sending; the agent cannot send, and a value it could not source stays empty. **Acceptance evidence:** from a CRM-fake deal and email-fake thread, the agent drafts an envelope whose prefilled values each cite their source; the sender edits one and sends; a request with no matching template is refused with a reason.

- **AGENT-002 — Fill what is missing.** *What the agent does:* before sending, detects missing parties, fields, attachments and inconsistencies (an entity name that differs from the CRM, a missing signer title, a W-9 not attached) and asks the right person (sender, colleague or recipient) by email with one specific question, then merges the answer with its provenance. *Data:* the draft envelope, CRM record and replies. *Human control:* the sender approves who may be asked; changes to material terms are flagged for the sender and never applied by the agent. **Acceptance evidence:** a draft missing the client signer's title and the W-9; the agent asks the right people, merges both answers with sources, and flags a reply that changes the price instead of applying it.

- **AGENT-003 — Chase stalled signers under the sender's rules.** *What the agent does:* watches each recipient's events (not opened, opened and left, bounced, out-of-office replies, repeated views), tells apart an unavailable signer, a bad address and a substantive objection, and follows up by allowed channels and times with a message suited to the cause; an objection goes to the sender (or Locket) instead of another reminder. *Data:* delivery and signing events, replies in the granted mailbox, the CRM owner. *Human control:* the sender sets channels, maximum contacts, quiet hours and the escalation owner; the agent never negotiates or changes the document. **Acceptance evidence:** three stalled signers (bounce, out-of-office until Monday, reply objecting to a clause); the agent proposes a corrected address, waits until Monday, and routes the objection to the sender, staying within the contact limit.

- **AGENT-004 — Verify the intended revision and parties.** *What the agent does:* before sending and at completion, compares the documents against the approved revision (Locket's approved version or the template version) page by page, checks every required party signed in the right capacity for the right entity, and checks every required field is complete and plausible. *Data:* the envelope's fingerprints and documents, the approved revision, CRM signatory data. *Human control:* a mismatch raises an exception the sender must resolve; the agent cannot alter a completed record. **Acceptance evidence:** an envelope whose second page differs from the Locket-approved revision is stopped before sending with the differing text; a completed envelope signed by a person without signing authority raises an exception and the downstream handoff waits.

- **AGENT-005 — Complete the downstream work.** *What the agent does:* after a verified completion, moves the CRM deal stage, files the record to Locket and the file store, and creates the next task (customer onboarding, invoice draft in Nectar or Pantry, HR onboarding in Willow), each once, with a receipt per effect. *Data:* the completed record and the standing grants. *Human control:* standing grants name the systems, fields and task types; the agent never creates a charge or binds a contract; failed effects stay open and visible. **Acceptance evidence:** a completion produces one deal update, one filed record and one onboarding task through fakes; a replayed webhook creates nothing new; a refused grant leaves its effect open with the reason.

- **AGENT-006 — Keep integrations correct.** *What the agent does:* reconciles webhook deliveries and connector state against Inkwell's records, detects drift (a renamed CRM field, a moved folder, an expired token), proposes the mapping repair and proves a replay causes no duplicate. *Data:* webhook logs, connector configurations and the remote API state under grant. *Human control:* the owner approves scope changes and repairs; uncertain remote effects stay unresolved until confirmed. **Acceptance evidence:** rename a CRM field in the fake; the agent detects failed write-backs, proposes the new mapping, and after approval replays them without duplicates.

- **AGENT-007 — Keep templates current.** *What the agent does:* learns from senders' repeated corrections (fields always added, recipients always changed) and outside changes (a new IRS W-9 revision, a new company address) and proposes a template revision with its reasons. *Data:* envelopes sent from the template and their corrections. *Human control:* the template owner reviews the diff and publishes a new version (`TMPL-003`); nothing changes without them. The template maker opens suggestions on Templates. Three distinct envelopes with the same correction establish repetition; a replacement PDF recorded in a corrected envelope is a source revision to review even on its own. Fields, their placement and prepared values, role count and routing, and replacement PDF fingerprints are compared against the template version the envelope came from. Names and emails supplied to fill a role are per-send inputs, not reusable role changes. Suggestions cite each supporting envelope and its correction event hash and time and show the before and after definition and original PDF. They use only envelopes the maker can already read under `ADMIN-001`; a recorded replacement or reported address is never labeled externally verified. The maker explicitly reviews and publishes through the normal template validator, preserving other template settings and producing the next version (`TMPL-003`); reviewing alone changes nothing, a stale template or withdrawn source suggestion is refused, and repeated approval with one command ID publishes once. The published template records the maker, time, proposal ID and supporting event references. **Acceptance evidence:** ten envelopes where senders added the same field; the agent proposes adding it with the ten examples cited; the owner accepts and version 2 is published.

- **AGENT-008 — Signer assistant.** *What the agent does:* answers a signer's questions about what they are being asked to fill and sign, citing the page and passage, and explains each field in plain English or Spanish. *Data:* only the envelope's documents visible to that signer. *Human control:* the assistant gives no legal advice, cannot fill or sign, and a signer's questions are private unless they choose to send them to the sender. **Acceptance evidence:** a signer asks what the termination notice period is and receives the answer with its page cited; a request to sign for them is refused; the sender sees no question unless the signer sends it.

- **AGENT-009 — Inbound agreements.** *What the agent does:* reads agreements forwarded to the account's Inkwell address or arriving from other platforms for the business to sign, identifies who internally should sign, prepares the internal signing and tracks the counterparty's request. *Data:* the forwarded email and attachments. *Human control:* the named internal signer approves and signs; the agent never signs and never replies to the counterparty without the owner's grant. **Acceptance evidence:** forward a vendor NDA; the agent identifies the internal signer by role, prepares it for them, and after they sign returns the executed copy only when the grant allows.

## Honest boundaries

- **Notarization and qualified signatures.** Inkwell does not act as a notary, a remote online notarization platform or an EU/UK qualified trust service. Buyers who need notarized signatures use a partner such as Proof (Notarize) through an integration, and qualified electronic signatures through a qualified trust service provider. Inkwell's records state the signing profile used and never claim those assurances.
- **Identity verification.** Inkwell orchestrates identity checks (`IDV-004`) through Persona or Stripe Identity and keeps only their references; it does not verify ID documents itself.
- **Payments.** Inkwell never handles card or bank data and holds no balances; Stripe processes payments at signing (`INTEG-010`) and recurring billing starts in Nectar or the buyer's billing system.
- **Legal validity.** Inkwell provides ESIGN and UETA consent, disclosure and audit records and states what its certificate and seal prove; it does not give legal advice or guarantee that any agreement is valid. Exceptional agreements (wills, certain family-law and court documents, some real-estate filings) go to counsel or specialist services.
- **Contract lifecycle.** Drafting, negotiation, clause libraries, the contract repository and renewal and obligation tracking belong to Locket (or Ironclad); proposals, quotes and content libraries belong to Platter (or PandaDoc). Inkwell signs and hands off.
- **Heavy document work.** Document conversion and PDF sealing run in an isolated service with streamed object storage, not inside request handlers; that is engineering work recorded in `PREP-001` and `RECORD-003`, not a cut in scope.
- **Providers the buyer supplies.** Mail, SMS (US 10DLC registration through Twilio), identity, payment, AI and integration accounts are the buyer's or Inkwell's registered relays; Inkwell records each provider outcome and does not guarantee carrier or inbox delivery.
- **US first.** USD costs, US time zones and business days, English and Spanish, and ESIGN/UETA are the defaults; other jurisdictions' signature rules are not claimed until their own profile is written.
